# Academic Backing for the AARM DEFER "Expressibility Limit" Reading — An Organized Assessment (Including Non-Existence Findings)

## TL;DR
- **The reading is well-precedented in spirit but appears novel as applied to AARM specifically.** No one has publicly noted this tension in AARM itself; but the general structural claim — that a monitoring/decision system working from a finite observed record cannot, from inside that record, settle certain properties or represent its own incompleteness — has strong, named precedent in runtime-verification monitorability theory, and partial precedent in epistemic logic, the frame/qualification problem, selective prediction, POMDP theory, and a very close 2026 agent-governance paper by Marcelo Fernandez.
- **The strongest formal parallel is runtime-verification monitorability** (Bauer–Leucker–Schallhart LTL3 / RV-LTL, and the good-prefix/bad-prefix/monitorability literature): some properties cannot be settled on any finite prefix and must yield an "inconclusive/?" verdict — a near-exact analog of AARM's DEFER-as-inconclusive and of situation #3 (composite risk unclear given incomplete history). This is a genuine, tight mapping.
- **The stronger self-referential claim** — that a *past-accumulation* context C cannot *represent* "C is itself incomplete" or "safety depends on future/unobserved information" (situation #4) — is real but only *partially* backed: it is closest to (a) the qualification problem (McCarthy), (b) awareness-of-unawareness logics (Fagin–Halpern; Halpern–Rêgo), and (c) Fernandez's 2026 "epistemological limits of local observability." None of these is identical to the AARM reading; each backs one facet.

## Key Findings
1. **AARM-specific engagement with this tension: none found (explicit non-existence finding).** No citation, review, blog, or follow-up paper was located that notes the specific gap between AARM's FRAMEWORK deferral situations #3/#4 and its CONFORMANCE R3 triggers, nor any discussion of an expressibility limit in AARM's context formalism. Commentary on AARM to date is descriptive/promotional (CSA, Vanta, Noma Security, ResilientCyber) rather than critical.
2. **Runtime verification monitorability is the anchor parallel** for the weaker, more defensible half of the reading (DEFER = inconclusive verdict; some properties undecidable on a finite prefix). Strong, formal, named.
3. **The self-referential half** (a system cannot, from within its context model, express that its context is insufficient or that safety hinges on the not-yet-observed) is backed piecewise by the qualification problem, awareness logics, and a strikingly parallel 2026 agent-governance series (Fernandez) — but is *not* identical to any single established result.
4. **The "not yet acquired" vs. "may or may not be acquired in future" distinction** maps cleanly onto (a) the known-unknowns / unknown-unknowns distinction, (b) the epistemic-logic gap between explicit knowledge and awareness-of-unawareness, and (c) POMDP value-of-information reasoning, which presupposes a *known* space of resolvable uncertainty and cannot price uncertainty outside its state space.

## Details (organized by area, with strength ratings)

### Area 1 — Runtime verification / monitorability theory. **STRONG PARALLEL (weaker half of the reading).**
The canonical result: online monitors observe only finite prefixes of an execution and emit verdicts {⊤, ⊥, ?}, where ? ("inconclusive") means the observed prefix is insufficient to determine satisfaction or violation under any continuation. This is exactly AARM's DEFER = actions that "cannot be conclusively classified at evaluation time" when "available context is insufficient, ambiguous, or internally conflicting."
- **Key work:** Andreas Bauer, Martin Leucker, Christian Schallhart, "Runtime Verification for LTL and TLTL," *ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology (TOSEM)* 20(4), Article 14, 2011, pp. 14:1–14:64, DOI 10.1145/2000799.2000800. The paper "introduces a three-valued semantics (with truth values true, false, inconclusive) as an adequate interpretation as to whether a partial observation of a running system meets an LTL or TLTL property," and proves the set of monitorable properties "is strictly larger" than the union of safety and co-safety properties. The "ugly prefix" is one after which the verdict is permanently inconclusive (non-monitorable).
- **Monitorability:** A property is monitorable after a prefix if some finite continuation still leads to a good or bad prefix. Safety and co-safety are monitorable; liveness properties such as GFp ("p infinitely often") and the request/acknowledge property G(r → Fa) are non-monitorable. Bauer–Leucker–Schallhart's own gloss of the latter (in "A Brief Account of Runtime Verification," *Journal of Logic and Algebraic Programming*): "the request/acknowledge property G(r → Fa) which states that every request is finally acknowledged: any pending request might be acknowledged in the next observation, so a finite trace is never sufficient to prove a violation… the formula cannot be evaluated to true either for any finite trace." Empirically, the Monic-tool paper "Four-valued monitorability of ω-regular languages" (arXiv:2002.06737) reports that of the 97 well-formed LTL formulas in Dwyer et al.'s specification-pattern collection, "55 formulas are monitorable and 42 are non-monitorable."
- **Related authoritative treatments:** Bauer–Leucker–Schallhart, "Comparing LTL Semantics for Runtime Verification" (RV-LTL, four-valued); Havelund & Peled, "Monitorability for Runtime Verification" (RV 2023 tutorial), classifying properties by monitorability; the good-prefix/bad-prefix notion originating with Kupferman & Vardi.
- **Mapping to AARM:** Tight for FRAMEWORK situation #3 (composite risk unclear given *incomplete action history*) and R3(a)/(c). "DEFER" is functionally the "?" verdict. **However**, an important gap: RV theory says the *property* cannot be settled on the current prefix; it does *not* require the monitor to internally represent "my prefix is incomplete." The monitor emits ? by construction; it does not reason about its own incompleteness. So RV backs the *observable behavior* (defer/inconclusive) but not the *self-referential representational* claim.

### Area 2 — Epistemic logic / awareness / unknown unknowns. **PARTIAL PARALLEL (backs the self-referential half, partially).**
- **Frame problem & qualification problem (McCarthy & Hayes 1969; McCarthy 1977, 1980 "Circumscription").** The qualification problem is "the impossibility of listing all the preconditions required for a real-world action to have its intended effect." McCarthy's own motivating example (1977): "[T]he successful use of a boat to cross a river requires, if the boat is a rowboat, that the oars and rowlocks be present and unbroken, and that they fit each other. Many other qualifications can be added, making the rules for using a rowboat almost impossible to apply, and yet anyone will still be able to think of additional requirements not yet stated." This is the closest classical analog to AARM situation #4 ("actions whose safety depends on information not yet available"): one cannot enumerate in advance all conditions relevant to an action's safety. Strong conceptual resonance, but it is about *unbounded preconditions*, not about a context store's inability to represent its own incompleteness.
- **Awareness logics (Fagin & Halpern, "Belief, Awareness, and Limited Reasoning," *Artificial Intelligence* 34:39–76, 1988).** Distinguishes explicit vs. implicit knowledge via a syntactic awareness operator. Crucially, **Halpern & Rêgo, "Reasoning About Knowledge of Unawareness" (KR 2006, arXiv:cs/0603020; revisited in arXiv:0906.4321, 2009)** observe that "standard propositional logics for knowledge and awareness cannot express the fact that an agent knows that there are facts of which he is unaware without there being an explicit fact that the agent knows he is unaware of" — and they extend the logic with quantification precisely to make that expressible. This is the most precise formal statement of the *self-referential* difficulty at the heart of the AARM reading: representing "my representation is incomplete" is not expressible in the base formalism and requires an explicit extension. This backs the reading's core intuition — that a plain past-accumulation C has no term for "C is insufficient" — while also showing it is a *known, surmountable* representational limit, not an absolute impossibility.
- **Mapping:** Good for the conceptual claim; but note these are logics of *belief/knowledge*, not of a security-monitor's action-context store. The analogy is at the level of "representational expressiveness," which is legitimate but requires care.

### Area 3 — Decision-making under incomplete information. **PARTIAL / MIXED.**
- **Selective prediction / learning to defer / reject option.** Chow's rule (C.K. Chow, "On Optimum Recognition Error and Reject Tradeoff," *IEEE Trans. Information Theory* 16(1):41–46, 1970) formalizes abstention when posterior risk exceeds a reject cost; El-Yaniv & Wiener (2010) formalize selective classification; Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017) bring it to deep nets; Madras et al. / Mozannar & Sontag formalize "learning to defer" to an expert. This backs AARM's confidence-threshold trigger R3(c) and FRAMEWORK situation #1 (insufficient confidence) very well. **But** classic reject-option is a statement about being near a decision boundary given the *current* feature vector — it is "ambiguity rejection." It does not, by itself, model deferral justified by *expected future information*. The distinction the user draws ("not yet acquired" vs. "may be acquired in future") is exactly the line between ambiguity/novelty rejection and value-of-information reasoning — so selective prediction backs situations #1/#2 but *not* #4.
- **POMDPs and value of information (Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra, "Planning and acting in partially observable stochastic domains," *Artificial Intelligence* 101:99–134, 1998).** POMDPs explicitly represent state uncertainty as a belief distribution and can value information-gathering actions (e.g., the Tiger problem). This is the natural home for "defer to acquire more information." **Critical caveat that supports the AARM reading:** a POMDP can only reason about uncertainty *within its predefined state space* — it prices the value of resolving *known* unknowns, but has no representation for unknowns outside its state space. This is precisely the AARM point: a past-accumulation C (like a POMDP with a fixed state space) cannot represent dependence on information it has no variable for. So POMDP theory both provides the machinery *and* illustrates the boundary the reading identifies.
- **Known-unknowns vs. unknown-unknowns:** widely used but largely informal in the decision literature; the rigorous version lives in the awareness logics above.

### Area 4 — AI safety / agent monitoring. **LOOSE-TO-MODERATE PARALLEL; one very close 2026 paper.**
- **General "monitors can't see outside their observation model" theme** is present but mostly informal: scalable-oversight limits (Amodei et al. "Concrete Problems in AI Safety" 2016; Greenblatt et al. "AI Control" 2023 on trusted/untrusted monitoring and sandbagging), and pieces like "On monitorability of AI" (*AI and Ethics*, 2024). These make the practical point but do not formalize the context-self-incompleteness claim.
- **Closest match — the Fernandez "Agent Governance Series" (2026).** Marcelo Fernandez, "From Admission to Invariants: Measuring Deviation in Delegated Agent Systems" (Paper 2 of a 6-paper series; arXiv:2604.17517; DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19672589; 2026), cited by companion papers under the descriptive title "…Epistemological limits of local observability in agent governance." Its central result, the **Non-Identifiability Theorem (Theorem 3.2)**, proves A₀ ∉ σ(g): under a "Local Observability Assumption" (the enforcement signal g decomposes as g(τ) = h(V(τ)), where V evaluates only point-wise constraint violations, independent of global trajectory properties), "the σ-algebra generated by the enforcement signal g does not contain A₀" — i.e., certain violations are undetectable from the local signal. Companion Theorem 3.5 (Monotonic Hidden Drift) shows "behavioral deviation from A₀ can grow arbitrarily while producing no enforcement signal whatsoever." The companion **Reconstructive Authority Model (RAM, arXiv:2604.22898; DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19669430)** summarizes Paper 2 as proving "for any governance architecture with finite memory and local observation, there exists a violation path that is undetectable until it has already caused a constraint breach," and its "Self-Justification Boundary" (§3.2) states that execution authority "depends on the system's ability to correctly describe the conditions under which it is allowed to act… This is not a failure of enforcement, but of epistemic completeness." RAM further defines a "coverage envelope" with an explicit "acknowledged unobservable residual δ(t)," arguing (Remark 4.1) that making δ(t) explicit "forces system designers to acknowledge what they do not know at each execution step, rather than silently assuming the provable state is complete." Paper 0 of the series ("Atomic Decision Boundaries," arXiv:2604.17511) separately states that its decision function "does not have predictive access to future states produced by environment actions that have not yet fired" — a direct echo of AARM situation #4.
  - **Assessment:** This is the single closest published parallel to the AARM reading — a formal, contemporaneous, same-domain (agent runtime governance) statement that a local/finite-observation governance system faces a permanent, structural gap between what it can observe/prove and the real state, and that this gap must be *explicitly represented* rather than assumed away. **Two important qualifications:** (1) Fernandez frames it as an *external* structural impossibility (the signal's σ-algebra cannot contain A₀; provable state Sₚ ⊊ real state Sᵣ), *not* as a Gödel-style claim that the system cannot represent its own incompleteness *from within*. (2) Fernandez does **not** cite AARM, and AARM does not cite Fernandez — despite near-identical subject matter and AARM predating the series (AARM submitted 10 Feb 2026; the Fernandez series Mar–Apr 2026). So this is a convergent, independent parallel, not a direct engagement.

### Area 5 — Self-reference / computation limits. **WEAK / OVERREACH RISK — do not lean on this.**
Gödel incompleteness, Tarski's undefinability of truth, and the liar paradox are frequently invoked for "a system cannot fully describe itself." They are *thematically* suggestive of the self-referential half of the reading, but the mapping is loose and easy to overstate: Gödel/Tarski concern provability/truth in formal arithmetic systems, not a finite append-only action log's ability to flag its own insufficiency. There is no rigorous result that transfers directly. Recommend citing this only as distant conceptual color, explicitly flagged as non-rigorous, and not as "backing."

### Area 6 — Belief revision (AGM). **WEAK / TANGENTIAL.**
AGM (Alchourrón, Gärdenfors & Makinson, 1985) models how a belief set is revised when new information arrives (expansion/revision/contraction; the "success" postulate). It is relevant background for "context grows as information arrives," and one notable AGM limitation is directly on-point: AGM "does not capture any of the information that has come to be true in virtue of the revision procedure itself… the agent is never informed about her epistemic state." That is a genuine echo of the AARM reading (the formalism has no term for statements about its own epistemic state). But AGM is about *rational belief change*, not about a monitor representing its own incompleteness or future-dependence, so treat as a supporting analogy, not a backbone.

## Recommendations (for the documentation record)
1. **State the reading as two separable claims and rate them separately.**
   - *Claim A (weaker, well-backed):* "DEFER is the inconclusive verdict for actions whose safety cannot be settled from the current finite context." → **Strongly precedented** by monitorability theory (Bauer–Leucker–Schallhart LTL3/RV-LTL; monitorability; good/bad prefixes). Cite this as the primary anchor.
   - *Claim B (stronger, partially backed):* "A past-accumulation C cannot express that C is itself incomplete or that safety depends on future/unobserved information (situations #3/#4)." → **Partially precedented**; assemble from the qualification problem (McCarthy), awareness-of-unawareness (Halpern–Rêgo), the POMDP state-space boundary (Kaelbling–Littman–Cassandra), and Fernandez's local-observability non-identifiability result. No single source states it exactly.
2. **Record the AARM-specific non-existence finding explicitly:** no third party has published this critique of AARM; the closest same-domain formal treatment is Fernandez (2026), which is convergent but independent and does not reference AARM.
3. **Cite conservatively; flag the two weakest analogies (Gödel/Tarski; AGM) as illustrative only.** The user's instinct to reject forced analogies is correct here: monitorability and awareness-of-unawareness are the two citations that genuinely carry weight for the two halves of the reading.
4. **Benchmarks that would change this assessment:** (a) discovery of a peer review, RV/formal-methods write-up, or a v2.0 AARM revision (the spec's authors have announced v2.0 work on "stateful long-horizon task governance") that addresses the #3/#4 vs. R3 gap would move Claim A→"noted in AARM specifically"; (b) a formal paper proving a monitor's context store cannot represent its own incompleteness *from within* (a true self-referential impossibility, not merely an external observability gap) would upgrade Claim B from "partial" to "strong." As of July 2026, neither exists in the sources reviewed.

## Overall verdict
The AARM reading is **conceptually sound and better precedented than a naive reviewer might assume, but genuinely novel as an applied observation about AARM.** Its weaker half (DEFER = inconclusive-on-finite-prefix, situation #3) is essentially a restatement of a textbook runtime-verification result and should be presented as such. Its stronger half (a past-accumulation context cannot represent its own incompleteness or future-dependence, situation #4) is *legitimate and independently converged upon* (Fernandez 2026; qualification problem; awareness logics), but no established result matches it exactly, and it should be documented as a synthesized argument rather than a citation of settled theory. The reading is therefore best characterized as **"novel in application, partially precedented in theory"** — not folklore, not fully rigorous, and safely defensible if the two claims are kept distinct.

## Caveats
- AARM (arXiv:2602.09433) was submitted 10 Feb 2026 and is very recent; absence of critical engagement is expected and does not imply the reading is wrong — only that it is, as far as public sources show, novel as applied to AARM.
- The reading blends two structurally distinct ideas (finite-prefix undecidability vs. self-referential incompleteness). They are related but not identical; conflating them would be the main analytic risk. Monitorability backs the first; it does *not* back the second.
- Several supporting sources are themselves 2026 arXiv preprints (Fernandez series; various agent-governance papers) that may not be peer-reviewed; treat their formal claims as preprint-grade.
- The Fernandez Paper 2 has a title discrepancy (document header "Measuring Deviation in Delegated Agent Systems" vs. citation title "…Epistemological limits of local observability…"); both refer to the same work (arXiv:2604.17517).
- The AARM paper's authorship shows a minor cross-source inconsistency: the arXiv listing and aarm.dev attribute it to Herman Errico, while at least one third-party reference list ("Authorization Propagation in Multi-Agent AI Systems," arXiv:2605.05440) cites it as "Anonymous (2026)." This does not affect the substance of the reading but is worth noting for the record.
